# Provo A+ Core 1 changelog

## v1.0.2
- Fairer term questions: the wrong answers are now chosen to be the same kind of thing as the right one, so the answer no longer stands out. A question that would give its own answer away is skipped when the term can be asked another way.
- Clearer wording: "What is X?" is now "Which description fits this term?", and feedback lines name each term and give its definition as a full sentence.
- Acronyms are asked three ways instead of four. "Which acronym stands for..." was removed because the initials gave the answer away, and "What does X do?" is now "What is X?".
- Troubleshooting modules (09 and 10) now ask for the usual fix, from the problem's name and from a scenario, instead of asking you to name a symptom you just read. Existing progress is kept.
- Reworded 22 questions that ended in a sentence fragment, such as "Which service model?", and one definition that contained its own answer.

## v1.0.1
- Home art now rotates every 10 seconds: the motherboard, redrawn wide with its key parts labeled, then connectors, hard drive vs SSD vs NVMe, RAID levels, laser printing with common symptoms, a copper and fiber cable chart, and hypervisors with cloud service models.
- Sharper background photo: upscaled from the original to 2048 pixels wide and saved at higher quality. The old version was a small, heavily compressed image that looked pixelated on large desktop screens.
- Menu: Provo home now goes to the website's getting-started page, and a new Choose an exam item opens the exam picker.

## v1.0.0
- First full release of Provo A+ Core 1 for CompTIA A+ Core 1 220-1201. All 14 modules are open and cover every objective.
- **Acronyms:** 123 exam acronyms, asked four ways, with a Type it mode.
- **Ports & Protocols:** all 15 ports on the 220-1201 list, asked both ways with spaced repetition, plus a TCP and UDP reference.
- **Objective modules (03 to 10):** 231 terms in 33 topics, each asked as name it, define it, and spot it in a scenario. Close neighbors such as Cat 5e, Cat 6, and Cat 6a are offered against each other. Troubleshooting problems also have a usual-fix question.
- **Question Qualifiers:** 50 bench-tech questions, 10 each for FIRST, NEXT, BEST, MOST likely, and MINIMUM.
- **Exam-Style Drill:** 189 original exam-style questions across all five domains and 27 skills, in four styles: best fit, scenario, exhibit, and direct. One question in four in each objective module comes from this bank.
- **Performance-Based Questions:** seven simulations with a new variation every run: a help desk queue, two custom builds, a hardware monitor, power protection, printer service, a RAID array, and cabling and powering an access point.
- **Mini-Exams:** 212 questions in the pool, picked in proportion to the domain weights.
- **Cram Sheet:** ports, cables, RAID, boards and power, printers, networking, cloud, and a troubleshooting symptom map on one page.
- Wrong answers are written about as long as the right one, so answer length doesn't give the answer away, and answer positions are shuffled every time.
